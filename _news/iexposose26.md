## TDX

Over the course of the semester, our TDX students explored the complex workings of their deep-tech topics, identified users and stakeholders, developed prototypes, and exchanged ideas with experts from various fields. We are incredibly proud of what our TDX students have achieved.

Team Energy Efficient demonstrated how emerging algae production plants in the pharmaceutical and food industries can streamline and improve cultivation using GC-IMS through a traffic-light indication system. This enables plant supervisors to diagnose algae more easily and address potential issues more effectively.

Team Gray Roomers implemented SCERA technology in a catheter sensor to help medical staff detect infections early and support workflow organization on the hospital floor. In doing so, they help reduce the need for antibiotics and lower the risk of antibiotic resistance for patients with long-term catheters.

## CBI

Over the past two semesters, our CBI students have worked incredibly hard to tackle SDG 11: Sustainable Cities and Communities, developing ideas for a better and more liveable summer in Mannheim.

Team Sustainable Squares explored future scenarios for the city, spoke with experts from Mannheim’s infrastructure about the feasibility of their concept, and developed a dynamic physical prototype. They have come up with a solution that combines layered cooling strategies in one street canyon: retractable solar sails with flexible PV to cut daytime heat, a mycelium-based pavement that stores less warmth, and vertical greenery for evaporative cooling. Together these measures reduce heat buildup, speed nighttime cooling, and make summer in the city safer and healthier. 

## PDP

Team Hidden-Elements tackled the lack of knowledge about what materials are used in smartphones, focusing on mining materials and their impact on people and nature, emphasizing on awareness rather than pushing blame. You can not only test your knowledge about it, but you can also call the elements, right from your phone!

Team Trash Talkers helped people sort waste and therefore reduced our environmental impact as consumers! And they did that with minimal changes to already existing infrastructures. Take a look at “Trash-Path”, a modernization of underground waste disposal units in large residential complexes!

Huge thanks to our cooperation Partners, the Stadt Mannheim and Lukas Wagner from the University of Marburg for their incredible support, and TransforMA for making these impactful projects possible!
